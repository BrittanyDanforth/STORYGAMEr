The revenue stream nobody builds for

Creator Rewards pays roughly 5 R$/day per "Active Spender" who plays 10+ minutes — but only for the first three experiences they open that day. So two design consequences, both free money: build a reason to be the first thing they open (offline earnings that cap, a daily chest, a claim streak), and a reason to stay past minute ten (a chest that unlocks at 10:00, a session-length luck ramp). An idle-friendly game collects this on top of gamepass revenue. A twitchy PvP game mostly doesn't.

Teardown

Why the ball-drop game works

Strip the theme off and it's nine systems. Every one is a retention or monetization surface, and not one of them is hard to build.

2–5 second core action

Drop → land → number pops. Short enough to be a six-second clip, long enough to feel like an event.

Two RNG layers

Micro (every drop pays) plus macro (1-in-7.91B collectible). Short-term dopamine, long-term chase.

Exponential currency

K → M → B → T → Qa. Numbers inflate so progress always feels fast, even when it isn't.

A ~50-node skill tree

The tree screenshot is the marketing. Turns "buy upgrade" into "build a strategy".

Rebirth

Wipes progress, multiplies output. Converts a finished player back into a new player.

Index / collection

The only system that retains players who have already maxed everything else.

Passives & offline income

The D1 engine — and D1/D7 return behavior is exactly what 2026 discovery ranks on.

Overlapping timers

Mystery event 6:05. Luck x25 1:12. Tickets x2 5:26. You can never cleanly leave.

Auto-everything

Auto-spin, auto-drop. Removing the gameplay increases retention — and sells as a pass.

The smartest thing in those screenshots

ADD 3 MINUTES — R$45 — a micro-priced, infinitely repeatable product that extends a server-wide jackpot timer. It converts because it's cheap, because it's sold at a moment of urgency, and because the whole server watches you do it. That's status, not a purchase. The pattern to copy across all three games: the payer isn't buying power, they're buying the removal of a wait, or a visible moment in front of other people.

Constraints

The rules you build around

Not optional, and the most common reason a first game gets moderated into the ground after it starts working.

Simulated gambling is banned. Chance-based rewards are not.

  • NOWagering currency on an outcome where the stake can be lost. No bet-size input, no poker, no blackjack, no roulette-with-bets. Unplayable casino scenery is still fine.
  • OKA chance-based reward. If the action is free — or costs a non-purchasable, regenerating resource — and always returns something, it's a reward mechanic, not a wager.
  • DORun two currencies: a regenerating, non-purchasable resource you act with, and a separate currency you earn. Never let a player spend earned currency for a chance at more earned currency.
  • DONever debit a persistent wallet on a loss. Risk only unbanked, in-run progress.
  • DOFrame every loss diegetically — the floor collapses, the guard catches you, the ball misses. Never "you lost your bet".
  • REQPaid random items must disclose odds. Since the global rollout in June 2026, every paid random outcome needs each result's probability shown as a percentage summing to exactly 100%, before purchase — including anything bought with in-game currency that Robux can buy, and including how a luck potion or luck pass numerically shifts the odds. Build the disclosure popup as a reusable component on day one and route every crate, egg, capsule and boost through it.
Concept 1 of 3 · the flagship

The three games

All three run on the same backend. They differ in the six-second clip they generate — which is the only thing that decides whether you get discovered at all.

PUSH A FORTUNE

Coin pusher + the full luck engine
Build effort
The six-second clip

A forty-coin avalanche pouring off the ledge in slow motion — with a rare capsule tipping over right at the end.

Why this one

Coin pushers already exist on Roblox and they're all toys — no rebirth, no luck stat, no index, no persistence. The arcade psychology is the most proven in the world: the near-miss is built into the physics, because there is always a coin teetering. Nobody has bolted the 2026 luck engine onto it, so you aren't fighting a dominant #1.

Core loop

  • Tokens regenerate — +1 per 3s, cap 50, accrues offline. Not purchasable, which is precisely what keeps the game policy-clean.
  • Drop tokens onto the shelf. The pusher slides on a 2.2s cycle. Coins fall off the front edge into Cash.
  • Rare Prize Capsules spawn on the shelf. Push one off the ledge to roll an Index collectible.
  • The pile persists across sessions — serialize ~120 coin positions. You log back in and it's exactly where you left it. Your progress lives in the world instead of in a number, which is an unusually strong return hook.
  • A shared MEGA PUSHER in the plaza: everyone feeds one machine, everyone watches one jackpot meter, payout splits by contribution. A whale's spending becomes physically visible to the entire server.

The one hard part

Physics on mobile. Hard-cap coins per machine at ~120 parts and absorb the oldest into pile value; drive the pusher with TweenService/CFrame rather than a physics constraint so it stays deterministic; simple cylinder coins with tuned CustomPhysicalProperties; one mega-pusher per server, not per player.

Policy

Clean. Tokens regenerate and can't be bought, every drop returns value, and the only paid random surface is the capsule roll — which routes through the odds popup.

ROLL A GIANT

Katamari on Roblox, with theft drama
Build effort
The six-second clip

You roll over an entire building — then eat another player, and their whole ball joins yours.

Why this one

Roblox has no serious katamari, and the mechanic is proven at enormous scale on mobile. More importantly, theft drama is the strongest viral engine on Roblox right now — the entire "Steal a ___" wave runs on it. This makes theft physical and spectacular instead of a UI interaction. It also needs no art budget: the money-shot is scale, which is free.

Core loop

  • Three-minute rounds, 12 players, one compact city arena around 300×300 studs.
  • Touch anything at or below your absorb tier and it sticks. You grow.
  • At 1.25× another player's mass you can absorb them and take their mass. That's the clip, the rage-quit, and the reason they re-queue.
  • Round ends, mass converts to Cash at a multiplier, leaderboard moment.
  • The lobby holds the entire luck engine — upgrades, rebirth, index, quests. Rounds generate the content; the lobby does the retention and the monetization.
  • Round-based scoping is the trick that makes this buildable: props reset every round, so there's no world persistence, no streaming, and no save complexity in the arena at all.

THE VAULT

Press-your-luck — cheapest to build, loudest clips
Build effort
The six-second clip — you want both kinds

The x900 cash-out. And the run that dies at x899. Loss clips travel further than win clips.

Why this one

The highest emotional amplitude per line of code on this list. No physics, no map, no combat — one room prefab, three doors, a multiplier counter and a particle burst. A working version is a weekend; everything else is the shared engine you already built.

Core loop

  • Entering is free. You descend floors.
  • Each floor offers three doors. Behind them: a multiplier bump, a bonus, or the ALARM.
  • Multipliers compound against your run haul. Bank at any moment and it goes to your wallet.
  • Alarm means caught — the run's haul is gone, and your wallet is never touched.
  • Deeper floors pay fatter multipliers at a higher alarm rate. The luck stat shifts door odds. Rare loot on floors feeds the Index.
  • Daily Vault — one free run per day at a 5× base. That single feature is the "be their first game of the day" hook, which is also what farms Creator Rewards.

The one hard part

There isn't one. This is the lowest-effort build on the list by a wide margin — which is exactly why it should ship first.

Policy — needs care, and the care is easy

Entry is free, so there is no stake. Never debit the persistent wallet, never offer a bet-size selector, and present the loss as being caught by security rather than losing a wager. Under those rules this is a roguelike run — the same as dropping carried loot on death in any dungeon crawler.

Foundation

Build the engine once

All three games are the same backend with a different three-second action bolted on top. Build this first and each additional game costs weeks instead of months.

ProfileStore saves

Session locking, no data loss. No exceptions and no homemade DataStore wrapper.

Big-number module

Mantissa + exponent. You pass 253 fast, and native Luau numbers silently corrupt currency once you hit quadrillions.

Server-authoritative RNG

The client never rolls, never decides a payout, never reports a result. It requests; the server rolls; the client animates what it's told.

Rate-limited remotes

Uncapped RemoteEvents are how games in this genre get gutted within a week of getting popular.

Content as data

Upgrades, rarities, zones and index entries live in module tables, so a content update ships in ten minutes — which is what holds the algorithm.

Odds-disclosure component

One reusable popup, wired into every random purchase in every game. Build it before you need it.

Mobile-first HUD

Most of your players are on phones. Design at phone size, check thumb reach, then scale up — never the reverse.

Full-server playtest

On a mid-range phone, at max players, before launch. This is where the physics budget actually gets decided.

Launch loop

3–5 TikToks a day, money-shot in the first half second, flat specific promise as the overlay. Codes with every update. Near-daily updates for two months.

One thing about discovery

2026 discovery ranks return behavior — D1 and D7 — not CCU and not session length. Offline income, daily chests and streaks aren't retention nice-to-haves, they are literally the ranking input. Sponsored ads only seed that signal: you pay to get the first players in, and their D1/D7 and payer conversion is what the algorithm then promotes on. Micro-influencers at 10k–50k followers consistently beat big creators on ROI in this genre.

Sequence

What to build, in what order

This one is a sequence — each step exists to de-risk the next.

  1. 1 ~2 WEEKS

    Build the shared engine

    Saves, big numbers, upgrades, rebirth, index, offline income, odds popup, mobile HUD. Nothing player-facing yet.
  2. 2 ~1 WEEK ON TOP

    Ship THE VAULT first

    The cheapest possible test of the engine, the marketing pipeline and your update cadence — with real revenue and the loudest organic clips. Learn on the game you can afford to have fail.
  3. 3 ~3–4 WEEKS

    Then PUSH A FORTUNE as the flagship

    Best long-term retention of the three — persistent pile, a genuine idle loop, ASMR-adjacent watchability — and the least credible competition. This is the one aimed at holding 5k CCU.
  4. 4 ONLY IF FUNDED

    ROLL A GIANT last

    Highest ceiling, highest build cost, and the only one whose retention depends on a live player population already existing. Let the first two pay for it.
